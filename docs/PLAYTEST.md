# Playtest kit

A small, honest test with 3 to 6 children shows whether the game teaches anything. It takes about 40 minutes per child (or pair).

## Rules

- **A parent or guardian agrees first and stays nearby.** Explain what you're doing and that nothing is recorded about the child except the scores below.
- **No names in the results.** Use Child A, Child B…; write only age and class.
- **Photos or video only with the parent's written OK,** and never showing the child's face unless they agree.
- Don't help during play unless the child is stuck for more than a minute. Note where they got stuck: that's the most useful finding.

## Steps

1. **Before (5 min):** on a fresh device (or after clearing the site data), open **Astronaut Academy** and let the child do all 4 drills once. Write down the score for each drill (0 to 3).
2. **Play (20 to 25 min):** one Cadet mission (ages 7 to 9) or Commander mission (10 to 12). Watch quietly and note:
   - where they got stuck or confused
   - whether they read Pip's hints or skipped them
   - what they said out loud
   - whether they sheltered from the storm
3. **After (5 min):** the same 4 drills again. Answers are shuffled, so they can't just remember positions.
4. **Ask 3 questions (5 min):**
   - What was the hardest choice?
   - What would you tell a friend who is going to play?
   - Would you play again? (1 to 5)

## Be honest about the limits

With 3 to 6 children this isn't a scientific study. Say so: "In a small test with N children aged X to Y, Academy scores went from A to B on average." Report it even if the result is small or mixed, and fix what the children got stuck on.
