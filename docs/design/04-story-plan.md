# 14 Sols: the master story (single source of truth)

> **Status:** expanded to 6 crises and 12 decisions with the team's OK (15 November 2026). Part 3's marsquake has its text in the game; its pictures and the ending debrief come next.
> **Who reads this:** all three team members and any AI helping them. Everything the game shows, every decision, every number and every source is in this file. If your part and this file disagree, **this file wins**; change it only with the whole team's OK.

## 1. The game in one paragraph

You command a crew of three orbiting Mars after mapping its hidden ice. A meteoroid wrecks your ship. The only way home is an old, empty return rocket on the surface that can make its own fuel from Mars air and water. The planets line up for the trip home in **14 sols** (Mars days); after that the next chance is **26 months** away. You land, build a small base, and face **6 crises** with **2 decisions in each** (12 in total, each A or B). **You never see whether a decision was good or bad until the end.** Each decision quietly changes three hidden numbers (Fuel, Time, Health). At the end the numbers give **one of two outcomes: Success (home) or Fail (stranded)**, and the game shows exactly which of your decisions caused a fail.

## 2. Rules every part must follow

1. **Two outcomes only:** Success or Fail.
2. **No early judgement:** no score, meter, colour, sound or words that hint whether a choice was good or bad. Reactions only describe what happens in the scene.
3. **Every option costs something.** All-A fails, all-B fails (section 7).
4. **Real science:** every crisis shows at least one real NASA fact with its source (section 8).
5. **Ages 7-12:** short sentences, explain a science word once, no jargon on buttons.
6. **Two languages:** English (default) and everyday Bangla (style in section 9).
7. **Works offline, landscape first:** 1280×760, 1024×768 and 812×375 (phone sideways). No external images or fonts.
8. **Reduce motion:** no shaking or moving backgrounds when the setting is on.

### PART 1: The fall

#### Opening: the backstory *(six shots, a calm narrator)*
1. People have always dreamed of living on Mars. But to live there, you need water.
2. Mars does have water. It is frozen into ice, hidden under the red dust. But no one knew exactly where.
3. So a small ship called the Wayfarer flew across space to find it. The trip took seven months.
4. On board were three explorers: you, our Commander; Mei, the doctor; and Tunde, the engineer. And Pip, the ship's computer, kept them company.
5. For more than two hundred days, they circled Mars, scanning the ground with radar, marking every patch of hidden ice.
6. Now the ice map is finished. One day, it will tell families where to build their homes.

#### Prologue: aboard the spaceship *(art: `ship`)*
1. Pip: Good morning, {name}! Day 412 of our mission. We are orbiting Mars.
2. Pip: Tomorrow we start the long trip home to Earth.
3. CRACK! A tiny meteoroid, smaller than a pea, smashes into the ship at 10 kilometres a second.
4. Tunde: The main engine is dead! And an oxygen tank is leaking!
5. Pip: There is one way home. Years ago NASA landed an empty return rocket on Mars, ready for a crew like us. It can make its own fuel from Mars air and water.
6. Pip: But Earth and Mars line up for the trip home only once every 26 months. Our window closes in 14 sols. A sol is one day on Mars.

#### Crisis 1: The broken ship (sol 0, in orbit) *(art: `orbit`)*
Scene:
1. The oxygen tank hisses. Mars fills the window below.

**Decision 1: The leak**
- Question (Pip): The oxygen tank is leaking. What do we do?
- **A. Land right away** — *The leak keeps hissing until we touch down.*
  Reaction: We dive toward Mars. The hiss grows louder all the way down.
- **B. Patch the leak first** — *A spacewalk to fix it. It takes time.*
  Reaction: Tunde floats outside with a patch kit. The hiss stops, but the clock keeps ticking.

**Decision 2: Where to land** *(shown on a map of Mars with real places at their real positions)*
- Question (Pip): Where should we land?
- **A. Flat ground by the rocket** (Elysium Planitia) — *A safe landing, but the ice is far away.*
  Reaction: A soft landing on smooth red ground. The rocket stands right beside us.
- **B. The ice crater** (Milankovič Crater) — *Water for fuel nearby, but a rough landing.*
  Reaction: BUMP! BUMP! We land hard on the rocky crater floor. Ice glitters close by.
- Landmarks on the map (not landing sites): Olympus Mons, Valles Marineris, Jezero Crater, Hellas Basin.

Landing (art: `descent`, 3 short lines, no decision; the player holds the landing rockets to touch down):
1. Pip: Heat shield on! The air outside glows hot.
2. Pip: Parachute open!
3. Pip: Rockets firing... touchdown!

NASA fact: **Space Station crews have tracked down and fixed real air leaks in space.**

#### Building the base (sols 1-3, guided, no decisions)
Each sol the player builds two things; a crew member explains why, with one real NASA number.

| Sol | Build | Explained by | Real NASA number |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Habitat | Mei | Curiosity measured the air dropping to −75 °C at night |
| 1 | Oxygen maker | Pip | MOXIE made 122 g of oxygen, about what a small dog breathes in 10 hours |
| 2 | Solar panels | Tunde | Mars is 1.5 times as far from the Sun as Earth; sunlight is less than half as strong |
| 2 | Battery bank | Tunde | A sol lasts about 24.6 hours |
| 3 | Water tanks | Mei | MRO found cliffs of buried water ice |
| 3 | Fuel plant | Pip | Mars air is mostly carbon dioxide |

The fuel line to the rocket is left unconnected (Part 2 needs it).

### PART 2: Surviving Mars

#### Crisis 2: The dust storm (sol 4) *(art: `dustStorm`)*
Scene:
1. Sol 4. The sky turns orange-brown. A dust storm is rolling over the whole planet.
2. Tunde: The solar panels are covered. They make almost no power.

**Decision 3: Who gets the power**
- Question (Pip): There is only enough power for one thing. What gets it?
- **A. The heaters** — *The crew stays warm. The fuel plant waits.*
  Reaction: The base stays warm. The fuel plant goes quiet.
- **B. The fuel plant** — *More fuel, but nights drop to −75 °C.*
  Reaction: The fuel plant hums. Mei wraps everyone in blankets as frost grows on the windows.

**Decision 4: The dusty panels**
- Question (Pip): The panels are buried in dust. What now?
- **A. Wipe them in the storm** — *Go out into the dust with a brush.*
  Reaction: Someone steps into the swirling dust with a brush. The wind pushes hard.
- **B. Wait it out** — *Stay safe inside until the dust settles.*
  Reaction: We wait inside and watch the dust race past. Two sols slip away.

After the storm *(art: `dustClear`)*:
1. When the storm passes, a gust of wind sweeps across the base and blows some dust off the panels.
2. Tunde: Lucky! Wind did the same for NASA's Spirit rover. It cleaned its panels more than once.

NASA fact: **In 2018 a dust storm covered all of Mars. It blocked the sunlight that NASA's Opportunity rover needed for power, and the rover never woke up again.**

#### Crisis 3: Bad air (sol 6) *(art: `badAir`)*
Scene:
1. Sol 6. Inside the base, everyone feels tired, and heads start to ache.
2. Mei: It's carbon dioxide, the gas we breathe out. It is building up in here.
3. Tunde: The CO₂ cleaner is clogged with storm dust. It needs more power to keep up.

**Decision 5: Power for the CO₂ cleaner**
- Question (Pip): Where does the CO₂ cleaner get its power?
- **A. Take power from the fuel plant** — *Clean air now. Less fuel made today.*
  Reaction: The fuel plant slows down. The cleaner whirs, and the headaches fade.
- **B. Open the spare oxygen** — *More oxygen, but the CO₂ stays.*
  Reaction: Fresh oxygen hisses in. The fuel plant keeps humming, but the air still feels stuffy.

Then:
1. Pip: There is another problem. Mars dust has perchlorate in it, a chemical that is bad for people. It comes in on our suits.

**Decision 6: Dust on the suits**
- Question (Pip): What do we do about the dust on our suits?
- **A. Clean every suit** — *Brush them off in the airlock every time. It takes time.*
  Reaction: Everyone brushes their suit in the airlock. Every trip outside takes longer.
- **B. Skip the cleaning** — *Save time. Some dust gets inside.*
  Reaction: We hurry inside. A thin red film settles on the floor.

NASA facts:
- **On the Space Station, astronauts have had headaches when carbon dioxide builds up, so NASA keeps it very low.**
- **NASA's Phoenix lander found perchlorate in Mars soil. NASA says large amounts could harm astronauts if breathed in or swallowed.**

#### Crisis 4: The solar storm (sols 7-8)
Sol 7, the warning *(art: `warning`)*:
1. Sol 7. A message from Earth arrives. It was sent 12 minutes ago, because radio takes that long to reach Mars.
2. Earth: "A huge cloud just blasted off the Sun at 2,650 kilometres a second. Its fastest particles reach Mars tomorrow."
3. Pip: Fast particles are radiation. Walls and water slow them down.
4. Pip: Light from the Sun reaches us in minutes. The particles come next, and the big cloud a few sols later. We have one sol to get ready.

Sol 8, the storm *(art: `solarStorm`)*:
1. Sol 8. The particles arrive. Pip's radiation counter clicks faster and faster.

**Decision 7: Shelter or finish the job**
- Question (Pip): The fuel line isn't connected yet. What do we do?
- **A. Everyone to the shelter** — *Safe inside. The fuel line waits.*
  Reaction: We squeeze into the shelter and shut the door. Outside, the fuel line lies unfinished.
- **B. Finish the job outside** — *Connect the fuel line first, then shelter.*
  Reaction: Tunde's radiation badge beeps faster and faster as he tightens the last bolt.

**Decision 8: A water wall**
- Question (Pip): Water blocks radiation. Should we build a water wall?
- **A. Stack the water tanks** — *A shield around the shelter. The water gets used up.*
  Reaction: We stack the water tanks around the shelter like bricks.
- **B. Keep the water** — *Save it for making fuel.*
  Reaction: We leave the water tanks by the fuel plant.

NASA facts:
- **In September 2017 a real solar storm hit Mars, and NASA's Curiosity rover measured the radiation on the ground more than double.**
- **That storm is in NASA's space weather database, DONKI: a giant cloud left the Sun on 10 September 2017 at 2,650 kilometres a second.**

### PART 3: The way home

#### Crisis 5: The ground shakes (sol 11) *(art: `quake`, to be drawn; uses `launch` for now)*
Scene:
1. Sol 11. The ground shakes. Cups rattle, and dust falls from the ceiling.
2. Pip: Was that a marsquake, or a meteorite hitting nearby? NASA's InSight lander felt both.
3. Tunde: Ow, my wrist! And the fuel tank seal has a crack.
4. Mei: No doctor can fly here from Earth. On Mars, I am the hospital.

**Decision 9: Tunde's wrist**
- Question (Pip): Tunde is hurt. What should he do?
- **A. Tunde rests** — *Mei treats him. The work waits.*
  Reaction: Mei splints his wrist. Tunde rests while the others work slowly.
- **B. Tunde keeps working** — *A brace, and back to work. It may hurt more.*
  Reaction: Tunde straps on a brace and grits his teeth.

**Decision 10: The cracked seal**
- Question (Pip): The fuel tank seal is cracked. How do we fix it?
- **A. Fix the seal properly** — *Take it apart and do it right. It takes time.*
  Reaction: We drain the tank, fit a new seal, and fill it again.
- **B. A quick patch** — *Tape it now. A little fuel may leak.*
  Reaction: The patch holds, but a thin hiss of fuel escapes.

NASA fact: **NASA's InSight lander recorded 1,319 marsquakes, including quakes caused by meteorites hitting Mars.**

#### Crisis 6: Launch day (sol 13) *(art: `launch`)*
Scene:
1. Sol 13. The return rocket stands ready. Its fuel tanks are almost full. Almost.
2. Pip: Tomorrow the planets start to move out of line.

**Decision 11: The rock samples**
- Question (Pip): The rocket is heavy. What about the Mars rocks we collected?
- **A. Leave them behind** — *A lighter rocket needs less fuel.*
  Reaction: We set the rock boxes down on the red ground. Maybe the next crew will take them home.
- **B. Keep them** — *Science is why we came.*
  Reaction: We strap the rock boxes in tight.

**Decision 12: When to launch**
- Question (Pip): Launch now, or wait one more sol for fuel?
- **A. Launch now** — *Go with the fuel we have.*
  Reaction: Countdown: 3... 2... 1...
- **B. Wait one more sol** — *More fuel, but less time.*
  Reaction: One more night on Mars. The fuel plant hums until morning. Then: 3... 2... 1...

NASA fact: **NASA's MOXIE experiment on the Perseverance rover made oxygen from Mars air, the first step toward making rocket fuel on Mars.**

#### Ending: Success *(art: `home`)*
1. The rocket roars up through the pink Mars sky.
2. Pip: We're in orbit! Next stop, Earth.
3. Pip: Here is the path you chose. *(show the 12 choices as a path)*

#### Ending: Fail *(art: `stranded`)*
1. Title: **Stranded**
2. Pip says the reason (one line, below).
3. Pip: These choices led here: *(list only the player's blamed decisions, by their option label)*
4. Buttons: **Try again** (back to the prologue, all choices cleared) and **Back to title**.

| Reason | Pip says |
|---|---|
| time | We ran out of time. The planets moved out of line for the trip home. |
| fuel | The rocket didn't have enough fuel to reach orbit. |
| health | The crew was too hurt or too cold to fly the rocket safely. |

#### Ending debrief *(next round)*
After the result, a debrief replays each decision with what really happens on Mars and the real number behind it, plus: a crew radiation dose meter (0.64 mSv per sol on the surface and 1.8 mSv per day in space, measured by Curiosity's RAD; NASA's career limit is 600 mSv), bone and muscle loss, eyesight changes (SANS), sleep and the 24.6-hour sol, and isolation and stress.

## 5. The hidden calculation

### Starting numbers
| Number | Meaning | Start |
|---|---|---|
| **Fuel** | How full the return rocket is | 5 |
| **Time** | Spare sols before the window closes | 7 |
| **Health** | The crew's health (cold, radiation, air, injuries) | 11 |

### What each decision changes, and why (the real basis)

| # | Option | Effect | Real basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A. Land right away | Health −2 | Losing cabin air means thin, low-oxygen air for the crew (Space Station leak, 2020) |
| 1 | B. Patch first | Time −2 | Spacewalks take hours of preparation and many hours outside |
| 2 | A. Flat ground | 0 | Safe and close to the rocket, but no water nearby |
| 2 | B. Ice crater | Fuel +3, Health −2 | Real buried ice cliffs on Mars (MRO, 2018); water plus Mars air makes fuel (MOXIE); rough ground is a hard landing |
| 3 | A. Heaters | 0 | Warm crew, no fuel made |
| 3 | B. Fuel plant | Fuel +3, Health −2 | Nights near −75 °C (Curiosity weather station) |
| 4 | A. Wipe the panels | Health −1 | Working outside in a storm is risky |
| 4 | B. Wait | Time −2 | Dust storms can last weeks (2018 storm) |
| 5 | A. Power from the fuel plant | Fuel −1 | Life support has to scrub CO₂ all the time (Space Station) |
| 5 | B. Spare oxygen | Health −2 | Extra oxygen does not remove CO₂; CO₂ build-up causes headaches (Space Station) |
| 6 | A. Clean every suit | Time −1 | Cleaning takes time on every trip outside |
| 6 | B. Skip the cleaning | Health −1 | Perchlorate in Mars dust is harmful if breathed in or swallowed (Phoenix; NASA) |
| 7 | A. Shelter | Time −1 | The job waits a sol |
| 7 | B. Finish outside | Health −3 | Radiation outside during a solar storm is far higher (Sept 2017 storm doubled surface radiation) |
| 8 | A. Water wall | Health +1; **Fuel −2 only if decision 2 was A** | Water is a good radiation shield (NASA); near the ice crater it can be refilled, on flat ground it can't |
| 8 | B. Keep water | Health −1 | No extra shield |
| 9 | A. Tunde rests | Time −1 | The crew is its own hospital; healing takes time |
| 9 | B. Tunde keeps working | Health −2 | Working on an injury makes it worse; no rescue from Earth |
| 10 | A. Fix the seal properly | Time −1 | A proper repair takes time |
| 10 | B. Quick patch | Fuel −1 | A leaking seal loses fuel |
| 11 | A. Leave rocks | Fuel +2 | A lighter rocket needs less fuel |
| 11 | B. Keep rocks | 0 | — |
| 12 | A. Launch now | 0 | — |
| 12 | B. Wait a sol | Time −2, Fuel +2 | More fuel made, less margin |

### The ending rule (checked in this order)
1. **Time < 0** → Fail, reason `time`
2. **Fuel < 7** → Fail, reason `fuel`
3. **Health ≤ 1** → Fail, reason `health`
4. Otherwise → **Success**

### Blame (which decisions to show on a fail)
Only list decisions **the player actually chose** from the list for the fail reason:
- **time:** 1B, 4B, 6A, 7A, 9A, 10A, 12B
- **fuel:** 2A, 3A, 5A, 8A (only when 2 was A), 10B, 11B, 12A
- **health:** 1A, 2B, 3B, 4A, 5B, 6B, 7B, 8B, 9B

## 9. Bangla style (all parts)

- Everyday spoken Bangla, the way people in Bangladesh talk.
- Technical words in English, written in Bangla letters: স্পেসশিপ, অক্সিজেন, ল্যান্ডিং, হিট শিল্ড, সোলার প্যানেল, ডাস্ট স্টর্ম, রেডিয়েশন, শেল্টার, ফুয়েল, রকেট.
- No bookish words (বিকিরণ, অবতরণ, মহাকাশযান, সৌরঝড়).
- Example: "ওহ নো! মেইন ইঞ্জিন বন্ধ হয়ে গেছে, আর একটা অক্সিজেন ট্যাংক লিক করছে!"
- Numbers in Bangla digits (১৪ সোল).
