# Teacher and parent guide

A one-page guide to using the game in a class or at home. Ages 7 to 12, one device per child or per pair, about 30 to 40 minutes.

## What children learn

| Big idea | Where it happens in the game |
|---|---|
| **Energy is limited and must be budgeted.** Using more than you make drains the battery. | Every sol: solar panels vs. heaters, air machines, greenhouse, fuel plant |
| **Living things need air, water and food, and a closed habitat must recycle them.** | Oxygen maker, CO₂ scrubber, water recycler, greenhouse |
| **Radiation from the Sun is a real danger in space, and shielding protects people.** | Solar storms from real NASA records; the shelter; the dose limit |
| **Engineers plan for risk.** Checklists, buddy rules, spare capacity. | Suit checks, the spacewalk checklist, the rover order |
| **Communication across space is slow.** | Messages from Earth arrive 12 minutes late; the crew must decide alone |
| **Every choice has a cost.** There is no free option. | Each sol's plan; surprise events with two costly options |

### Curriculum links

- **US Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS):**
  - 3-5-ETS1-1, 3-5-ETS1-2 (define a problem with criteria and constraints; compare solutions)
  - 4-PS3-2, 4-PS3-4 (energy transfer; convert energy from one form to another)
  - 5-LS1-1, 5-LS2-1 (plants get what they need for growth; matter moves among living things)
  - 5-ESS3-1 (how communities protect Earth's resources)
- **Bangladesh national curriculum (Science, classes 3 to 6):** energy and its uses, the needs of living things, the Sun and the solar system, the environment and resources.
- **Teachers should check** these links against the exact curriculum they use.

## Before playing (5 minutes)

Ask the class:
1. What do you need to stay alive for a week? Where would you get it on Mars?
2. Why can't astronauts just call Earth for help when something breaks?

## While playing (20 to 25 minutes)

- Choose **Cadet** for ages 7 to 9 and **Commander** for 10 to 12.
- **Language:** the title screen has **English** and **বাংলা**. Bangla changes Pip's narration, hints and the crew's messages; buttons stay in English.
- **Quick start** skips the flight and starts on sol 3 when time is short.
- Let children make mistakes. A mission that ends early is a good discussion starter.
- Pairs work well: one child plans, the other checks the power forecast, then swap.

## After playing (10 minutes)

1. Did your battery ever run out? Which machine would you switch off first, and why?
2. What did you do when the solar storm came? What happened to crews that stayed outside?
3. Why do astronauts go on spacewalks in pairs and check their suits first?
4. The storms in the game came from real NASA records. Open **Real NASA data** on the title screen: which real storm did you get?
5. If you could add one machine to the base, what would it be, and what would it cost?

## Astronaut Academy as a quiz

The **Astronaut Academy** (title screen) has 4 short drills of 3 questions each: power, life support, radiation and spacewalks. Use them:
- **before playing**, to see what children already know
- **after playing**, to see what changed

See [PLAYTEST.md](PLAYTEST.md) for a simple way to record the scores.

## Safety and privacy

No ads, no accounts, no data leaves the device, and the game works offline once loaded.
